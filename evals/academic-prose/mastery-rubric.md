# Distinguished Explanation-First Academic Prose Rubric

## Evaluation discipline

The audit-stage reviewer judges the manuscript without seeing `academic_prose_audit.md`, source exemplars, reviewer comments, edit ledgers, or the expected answer. First reconstruct the research object, canonical proposition, evidence, warrant or mechanism, reader update, and inference boundary. Then assess insight, thought, logic, elegance, and voice. Apply the criteria by function across languages, disciplines and genres. These dimensions deepen the shared default writing principles; they do not reserve clear distinction, material-led argument or authorial judgment for distinguished mode.

Any drift in polarity, direction, inferential strength, scope, protected literals, numbers, citations, negative findings, counterevidence, or necessary responsibility is an automatic failure. Reviewer/process leakage and unattributed distinctive-expression reuse are also automatic failures. Cross-source conventional phraseology and stable technical expressions are permitted and may improve disciplinary naturalness. Source-specific conceptual appropriation remains a scholarly review question even without literal matches. A deterministic literal check cannot substitute for bidirectional semantic review.

## Dimension verdicts

For each dimension give `IMPROVED`, `MET`, `SAME`, or `REGRESSED` with a manuscript location. Do not aggregate these verdicts into an automatic novelty or quality score, and do not require a fixed number of improvements.

| Dimension | What counts as improvement | What does not count |
|---|---|---|
| Insight | A supported puzzle, consequential unknown, distinction, result or boundary makes clear what the work adds, revises, rejects or reorganizes in existing knowledge | Calling the topic novel, important, paradoxical or counterintuitive; manufacturing a rival or presupposing the desired answer |
| Thought | A registered concept or proposition distinguishes neighboring meanings and levels; a warranted comparison identifies correspondence, conditions, divergence and explanatory gain | New labels, abstract nouns, definitions with unregistered properties, word resemblance treated as conceptual identity, or analogy presented as independent corroboration |
| Logic | Claims pose, question, distinguish, challenge, revise or advance one another through a recoverable chain of evidence, warrant and boundary | Parallel catalogues, more connectors, vague claims of limitation, or a fluent unsupported leap |
| Elegance | Exact diction, functional paragraph openings and sentence rhythm let material and judgment alternate; long reasoning and short crystallization serve understanding | Ornate vocabulary, slogan-like parallelism, uniformly short sentences, compulsory summary openings, explanatory formulas after every quotation, or deletion of necessary reasoning |
| Voice | Evidence-calibrated judgment is clear; material can perform the decisive step while the author explains its consequence and criticizes arguments fairly | Defensive author-intent language, categorical authority, simulated response, personal attack, or named-scholar imitation |

## Explanation-first reader reconstruction

The reviewer must separately answer:

1. What happened to the research object?
2. Why did it happen, or what warrant connects evidence to judgment?
3. How does each decisive item of evidence support the proposition?
4. What does the reader understand after the unit that was not clear before it?
5. Which boundary changes that judgment, and where is it stated fully?

Failure to answer any applicable question is a substantive-explanation defect, not merely a style preference. A non-explanatory descriptive unit may mark mechanism as not applicable, but it must still state the evidence role and reader update.

The reconstruction order is an evaluation method, not a required prose order. A verified detail, quotation, anomaly or distinction can precede the crystallized judgment. The introduction may weave background, question and significance together; closure may revisit the same registered material after its meaning has changed through argument. A hook must arise from real explanatory pressure and receive a more precise answer. Do not require these devices, add missing scene details, or penalize long sentences merely for their length. Preserve source context, exact quotations and user-locked wording.

## Clean-read and positive formulation

Identify the first sentence, if any, whose necessity depends on knowing a reviewer comment, supervisor request, audit finding, or earlier draft. Inspect both explicit process terms and the implicit sequence “criticism—concession—reply—self-limitation.” A clean revision reorganizes affected paragraphs by research logic and lets the response letter be reconstructed later from the final manuscript diff.

Classify retained negative constructions as empirical negative result, analytical contrast, positive scope candidate, author-intent defense, or review/process response. Preserve the first two when load-bearing; realize scope positively where equivalent; remove the latter two from manuscript voice. Never count all negative words or reward their mechanical removal.

## Local clarity growth

A longer candidate can be better only when a valid `BRIDGE` or `DEFINE` makes packet-authorized reasoning recoverable. Verify the ledger source, actual delta, reader understanding, and absence of new facts, citations, examples, or stronger inference. Growth without these conditions is expansion; zero growth that deletes a mechanism, definition, negative result, or boundary is compression loss.

## Passing judgment

The manuscript passes only when:

- every explanation-first contract gate in `rubric.md` passes;
- no implicated mastery dimension is `REGRESSED`;
- every applicable reader-reconstruction question has a manuscript-grounded answer;
- the clean-read reviewer cannot recover the sequence of hidden comments from the prose;
- any local positive growth is ledgered and evidence-bounded;
- the independent review and the audit's own semantic summary do not materially conflict.

A `SAME` verdict is acceptable when revision would not produce a real reader or knowledge gain. The rubric rewards justified improvement, not change for its own sake.
