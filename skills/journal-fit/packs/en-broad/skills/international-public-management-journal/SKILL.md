# International Public Management Journal (international-public-management-journal)

## Journal positioning

IPMJ is a Taylor & Francis (Routledge) journal of public management with an explicitly international and comparative remit, historically linked to the International Public Management Network community. It publishes research on public management and governance, comparative public administration, and organizational theory and organizational behavior in public organizations. Its editorial culture has become strongly research-design conscious: cross-national surveys, field and survey experiments in public organizations, and multi-country comparisons of management practices are its staple, and it is one of the main homes of behavioral public administration outside JPART. What wins here is a public-management question whose answer is shown to hold, or to vary in a theorised way, across countries or administrative systems. Readership is the international public-management research community, more European and Asia-Pacific in cases than PAR, and less reform-narrative than PMR.

This skill is a **fit / venue-selection / re-framing** tool. It does not replace the journal's current official submission guidelines. Before submitting, re-check the live instructions for authors on the Taylor & Francis site and the submission system it names.

## When to trigger

- The author names International Public Management Journal / IPMJ as the target venue, or wants an international public-management outlet with a comparative emphasis.
- A single-country public-management study needs re-framing so its cross-system relevance and theoretical portability are explicit.
- A behavioral or organizational-behavior paper in a public setting is choosing between JPART, IPMJ, PMR, and Public Administration.
- The author needs IPMJ's desk-reject risks and a credible public-management alternative list.

## Scope & topic fit

- Comparative public management: how management practices, reforms, and performance regimes operate across countries, levels of government, and administrative traditions.
- Organizational behavior in public organizations: motivation and public service motivation, leadership, job attitudes, turnover, red tape, and employee performance, often with experimental or multi-country survey designs.
- Public governance and collaboration, contracting and hybrid delivery, digital and data-driven management, and accountability in international and multilevel settings.
- Behavioral public administration: citizen–state interactions, administrative burden, performance information use, and decision-making of public managers.
- Theory-building and replication studies that test whether public-management findings travel beyond the Anglo-American cases where they originated.

## Method & evidence reporting boundary

- Report the active `empirical-research` core contract ID and revision and, as applicable, its registered estimand or theoretical target, assumptions, diagnostic dispositions, hash-bound Artifact IDs, and observed inference ceiling.
- Explain why those already-registered records matter to this venue's audience. This venue profile does not select estimators, clustering levels, thresholds, diagnostics, or repairs.
- If a required core record is absent or failed, return the design to `empirical-research` and report the resulting narrowed or withdrawn inference; do not repair it in the venue layer.
- Keep the venue-specific placement, data/code, disclosure, and transparency requirements below.

## Structure & house style

- The introduction states the public-management question, why the international or comparative setting matters to the answer, the design, and the contribution; a country study must say what it teaches readers in other systems.
- Position against the international public-management literature (including JPART, PMR, Public Administration, and Governance conversations), not only against the literature of the home country.
- Describe administrative context enough for a reader from another system: institutional terms defined, the level of government and the type of organisation stated, and the comparability of measures across countries addressed.
- Follow the Taylor & Francis conventions the journal adopts: check the live page for format-free or template submission, abstract length, keywords, and the required declarations (funding, disclosure of interest, data availability, generative-AI use).
- Expect double-anonymous review and prepare separate anonymous and full manuscript files; keep identifying acknowledgements and funding on the title page.
- Place robustness, instrument details, and multi-country appendices in supplemental material, and be ready to supply a data availability statement with a repository identifier where data are shared.

## Common desk-reject triggers

- Hiding, overriding, or rhetorically repairing a failed core diagnostic or observed inference ceiling instead of reporting the registered disposition.
- A single-country study with no argument for cross-system relevance, or a reform description with no public-management theory.
- A generic organizational-behavior or HRM paper in which the public sector is incidental context.
- An under-theorised multi-country dataset analysis that reports differences between countries without explaining them.
- A submission that ignores the journal's format, declaration, and anonymisation requirements.

## Re-routing decision

- Theory-first public management with identification emphasis → `journal-of-public-administration-research-and-theory`; practice-bridging US administration → `public-administration-review`.
- Public-service reform and delivery with a European centre of gravity → `public-management-review`; general and interpretive administrative scholarship → `public-administration-journal`.
- Institutions, executive politics, and the state → `governance-journal`; development-administration settings → Public Administration and Development; personnel-focused work → Review of Public Personnel Administration.
