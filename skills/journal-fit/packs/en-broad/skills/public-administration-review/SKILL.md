# Public Administration Review (public-administration-review)

## Journal positioning

PAR is the flagship professional journal of the American Society for Public Administration (ASPA), published by Wiley, and the field's most visible general outlet. Its defining trait is that it is written for scholars *and* practitioners at once: the journal describes itself as the only public-administration journal serving both audiences, and every research article is expected to distil "Evidence for Practice" takeaway points. What wins here is a question of field-wide significance to public administration, answered with a design defended on its own terms (quantitative, experimental, qualitative, mixed, normative, or a research synthesis), and translated into what a public manager should do differently. PAR is methodologically pluralistic and does not run a single identification template; it is less theory-first than JPART and less European-interpretive than Public Administration.

This skill is a **fit / venue-selection / re-framing** tool. It does not replace the journal's current official submission guidelines. Before submitting, re-check the live author instructions on the Wiley/ASPA site and the submission system.

## When to trigger

- The author names Public Administration Review / PAR as the target venue, or wants the ASPA flagship.
- A public-administration manuscript is rigorous but reads as purely academic; it needs a genuine research-to-practice bridge to fit PAR.
- A general public-management, public-policy, or public-personnel paper needs re-framing so the practitioner "so what" is real rather than decorative.
- The author needs PAR's desk-reject risks and a credible public-administration alternative list (JPART, Public Administration, PMR, IPMJ, Governance).

## Scope & topic fit

- Bureaucracy, governance, and the organization of the administrative state: agencies, oversight, discretion, administrative burden, representative bureaucracy.
- Public management and performance: performance measurement and management, contracting and collaboration, networks, coproduction, public-sector innovation and digital government.
- Public personnel and organizational behavior in the public sector: public service motivation, leadership, turnover, diversity and equity, employee well-being.
- Public budgeting, finance, and financial management at federal, state, and local levels.
- Policy implementation, program evaluation, emergency and crisis management, and international/comparative administration that yields lessons usable across systems.
- Normative theory on value questions in public administration, plus research syntheses and meta-analyses with a reproducible search strategy.

## Method & evidence reporting boundary

- Report the active `empirical-research` core contract ID and revision and, as applicable, its registered estimand or theoretical target, assumptions, diagnostic dispositions, hash-bound Artifact IDs, and observed inference ceiling.
- Explain why those already-registered records matter to this venue's audience. This venue profile does not select estimators, clustering levels, thresholds, diagnostics, or repairs.
- If a required core record is absent or failed, return the design to `empirical-research` and report the resulting narrowed or withdrawn inference; do not repair it in the venue layer.
- Keep the venue-specific placement, data/code, disclosure, and transparency requirements below.

## Structure & house style

- The introduction states the administrative problem, why it matters beyond one agency or country, the approach, and the headline finding early; PAR readers span specialties, so the framing is field-wide, not subfield-internal.
- Write in plain professional English: technical material set off, jargon minimised, acronyms spelled out on first use, no first-person narration in research articles.
- Draft the 3–5 sentence-length **Evidence for Practice** points alongside the contribution, and tie each to a confirmatory result rather than an exploratory one; the journal's guidance has long encouraged them at submission and required them at revision.
- Author–date (APA-style) referencing; a short abstract on a page that carries no author identification, because the journal is prepared for double-blind review.
- PAR is a signatory of the Transparency and Openness Promotion (TOP) guidelines: cite data and materials, share quantitative data via Dataverse and qualitative data via the Qualitative Data Repository where appropriate, document design and analysis decisions in a supplementary file following the relevant reporting standard, and note that the journal takes no strong position on pre-registration but recognises it with badges.
- Practitioner-authored or co-authored research is welcome; shorter practice-facing pieces have historically been routed to the Viewpoint feature rather than to research articles. The older commissioned "Evidence in Public Administration" and "Perspectives" features were folded into ordinary articles from 2018; the evidence-to-practice orientation now lives in the Evidence for Practice points.

## Official-submission checklist

- Before giving submission-ready advice, read `../../resources/source-basis.md` and `../../resources/official-source-map.md`; start from the official source anchors for this journal family, then cite the current journal-specific page you checked.
- Search the live site for "Public Administration Review author guidelines / information for contributors" and follow the current Wiley/ASPA version, not a third-party copy.
- Re-check the live submission portal (Editorial Manager versus Research Exchange), double-blind preparation and separate title page, the current word and abstract caps, article-type menu, Evidence for Practice wording, APA edition, and the data-availability / TOP statement.
- Re-check ethics/IRB disclosure, conflict-of-interest and funding statements, and any open-access APC only if choosing open access.
- If the live official instructions conflict with this skill, the official instructions win.

## Pre-submission self-check

- [ ] One sentence stating why this paper matters to public administration as a field and to the practitioners who read PAR.
- [ ] The applicable core contract ID/revision, hash-bound diagnostics, and observed inference ceiling are reported without venue-layer alteration.
- [ ] The introduction positions the paper against the current public-administration conversation on this question, not only against one subfield.
- [ ] Evidence for Practice points are drafted, honest, and traceable to specific results.
- [ ] Manuscript is anonymised, references and endnotes are counted toward length, and data/materials citations plus the TOP-style statement are ready.

## Common desk-reject triggers

- Hiding, overriding, or rhetorically repairing a failed core diagnostic or observed inference ceiling instead of reporting the registered disposition.
- A technically sound but purely academic finding with no credible implication for practice, or Evidence for Practice points bolted on that do not follow from the analysis.
- A descriptive single-agency or single-program account with no lesson that travels across jurisdictions.
- A policy-analysis or political-science paper in which administration is scenery rather than the object of study.
- Jargon-dense prose written for a subfield, or an under-anonymised manuscript.

## Re-routing decision

- Theory-first, identification-heavy public management with implications for theory rather than practice → `journal-of-public-administration-research-and-theory`.
- European or interpretive/critical administrative scholarship → `public-administration-journal`; reform and management-practice studies → `public-management-review`; explicitly cross-national management → `international-public-management-journal`.
- Comparative institutions, executive politics, and the organization of the state → `governance-journal`; a policy-analysis core → Journal of Policy Analysis and Management or Policy Studies Journal.
- Chinese-language public administration → the `chinese-public-administration` deep pack.
