# Public Management Review (public-management-review)

## Journal positioning

PMR is an international public-management journal published by Taylor & Francis (Routledge) and the leading outlet of the European-rooted public-management community that gathers around the IRSPM conference circuit. It publishes "substantive papers contributing to the deepening of theory about public management and/or our empirical knowledge of public management", and its editorial taste favours the study of public-service reform and delivery: new public management and its successors, public governance and networks, public value, co-production and public service logic, collaborative innovation, digital transformation, and hybrid and third-sector organisations. Compared with JPART it is more reform- and practice-oriented and more European in cases and theory; compared with PAR it is more explicitly a management journal and less US-institutional. Quantitative, qualitative, mixed, and conceptual-theoretical papers all appear, and international special issues are a large part of its identity.

This skill is a **fit / venue-selection / re-framing** tool. It does not replace the journal's current official submission guidelines. Before submitting, re-check the live instructions for authors on the Taylor & Francis site and the T&F Submission Portal.

## When to trigger

- The author names Public Management Review / PMR as the target venue, or wants the European public-management flagship.
- A study of a public-service reform, delivery model, or management innovation needs re-framing so it deepens public-management theory rather than describing a programme.
- A PA paper with a strong management and organisational core is choosing between PMR, IPMJ, Public Administration, and JPART.
- The author needs PMR's desk-reject risks and a credible alternative list.

## Scope & topic fit

- Public-service reform and delivery models: NPM and post-NPM, new public governance, agencification, marketisation, hybridity, and their organisational consequences.
- Co-production, co-creation, public service logic, citizen and user engagement, and public value creation and measurement.
- Collaboration, networks, partnerships, and innovation across public, private, and nonprofit sectors; collaborative and social innovation.
- Digital government and data-driven public management, performance and accountability regimes, and public-sector organisational behaviour (leadership, motivation, well-being).
- Comparative and international public management, including non-Western and emerging-economy settings, and theory-building papers that synthesise or reframe public-management concepts.

## Method & evidence reporting boundary

- Report the active `empirical-research` core contract ID and revision and, as applicable, its registered estimand or theoretical target, assumptions, diagnostic dispositions, hash-bound Artifact IDs, and observed inference ceiling.
- Explain why those already-registered records matter to this venue's audience. This venue profile does not select estimators, clustering levels, thresholds, diagnostics, or repairs.
- If a required core record is absent or failed, return the design to `empirical-research` and report the resulting narrowed or withdrawn inference; do not repair it in the venue layer.
- Keep the venue-specific placement, data/code, disclosure, and transparency requirements below.

## Structure & house style

- The introduction states the public-management problem, the theoretical lens, and the contribution to public-management theory or evidence; reform descriptions without a theoretical question do not survive editorial screening.
- Format-free submission: any consistent scholarly reference style and layout at first submission; the journal style is applied after acceptance. Spelling may be US or UK if consistent.
- Compact main text: the initial-submission cap excludes references and appendices but includes tables and figures, and over-length papers are not sent to review; essential appendices are published online only.
- An unstructured abstract of 150 words, optionally accompanied by a 150-word abstract in another language; two to five keywords; short author biographies; CRediT contributor roles at submission.
- Two manuscript files for double-anonymous review (with and without author details); once submitted, the author team cannot be changed except on a reviewer's explicit recommendation.
- Declarations expected at submission: funding, disclosure of interest, a generative-AI use statement (including a statement that none was used), and a data availability statement with a persistent identifier where data are deposited.

## Verified submission facts (live-checked 2026-09-02; re-check before submitting)

- Publisher & submission system: Taylor & Francis; submissions via the Taylor & Francis Submission Portal at https://rp.tandfonline.com/submission/create?journalCode=RPXM (source: https://www.tandfonline.com/action/authorSubmission?show=instructions&journalCode=rpxm20, page marked "Updated 01-07-26").
- Review model: after editorial assessment the paper "will then be double anonymous peer reviewed by independent, anonymous expert referees"; the journal's about page describes initial editorial screening followed by a minimum of double anonymized peer review (sources: https://www.tandfonline.com/action/authorSubmission?show=instructions&journalCode=rpxm20; https://www.tandfonline.com/journals/rpxm20/about-this-journal).
- Word limit: "Initial submissions to PMR must not exceed 8,000 words. This excludes references and appendices/annexes but does include Tables and Figures"; "Papers in excess of this length will not be accepted for review" (source: https://www.tandfonline.com/action/authorSubmission?show=instructions&journalCode=rpxm20).
- Fees / APC policy for standard submissions: "There are no submission fees, publication fees or page charges for this journal"; an APC applies only to optional Open Select open access; colour figures in print carry a charge (source: https://www.tandfonline.com/action/authorSubmission?show=instructions&journalCode=rpxm20).
- Data / replication policy: the journal applies the Taylor & Francis Basic Data Sharing Policy — authors are encouraged (not required) to share data in a recognised repository, to cite data sets, and to provide a Data Availability Statement; a deposited data set's DOI is requested at submission (source: https://www.tandfonline.com/action/authorSubmission?show=instructions&journalCode=rpxm20).

## Pre-submission self-check

- [ ] One sentence stating which public-management theory or body of evidence this paper deepens, beyond the reform it studies.
- [ ] The applicable core contract ID/revision, hash-bound diagnostics, and observed inference ceiling are reported without venue-layer alteration.
- [ ] The introduction positions the paper against the international public-management literature, including its European strands.
- [ ] Main text with tables and figures is within 8,000 words; references and appendices are outside the count and appendices are trimmed to essentials.
- [ ] Anonymous and full manuscript files, abstract(s), keywords, CRediT roles, AI-use and data availability statements are all prepared.

## Common desk-reject triggers

- Hiding, overriding, or rhetorically repairing a failed core diagnostic or observed inference ceiling instead of reporting the registered disposition.
- A reform or programme description, however detailed, with no public-management theoretical question and no contribution beyond the case.
- An over-length initial submission, which the journal states will not be accepted for review.
- A generic management or private-sector organisational-behaviour paper with the public sector as context only.
- Missing required declarations (AI use, disclosure, data availability) or a compromised anonymous file.

## Re-routing decision

- Theory-driven public management with strong identification and implications for theory → `journal-of-public-administration-research-and-theory`; practice-bridging US public administration → `public-administration-review`.
- European general public administration, including interpretive and critical work → `public-administration-journal`; explicitly cross-national management comparison → `international-public-management-journal`.
- Institutions, executive politics, and the organisation of the state → `governance-journal`; nonprofit-centred work → Nonprofit and Voluntary Sector Quarterly; policy-process work → Policy & Politics or Journal of European Public Policy.
