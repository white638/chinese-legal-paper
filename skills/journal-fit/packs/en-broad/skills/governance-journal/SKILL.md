# Governance: An International Journal of Policy, Administration, and Institutions (governance-journal)

## Journal positioning

Governance is a Wiley journal published in association with the International Political Science Association's Research Committee on the Structure and Organization of Government (SOG). It sits at the intersection of comparative politics, public policy, and public administration: the journal describes itself as devoted to executive politics, public policy, administration, and the organization of the state, with particular interest in comparative work and an openness to any methodological approach. What wins is a novel, important claim about how states, executives, bureaucracies, or policy systems work, stated directly on the first pages and shown to travel beyond one case; articles about specific events, policies, or structures must carry general theoretical significance. Readership is global and mixed scholar-practitioner, and the editors have said they prioritise fundamental governance problems in large developing countries over incremental reforms in already high-performing Western administrations.

This skill is a **fit / venue-selection / re-framing** tool. It does not replace the journal's current official submission guidelines. Before submitting, re-check the live author instructions on the Wiley site and the journal's own editorial pages.

## When to trigger

- The author names Governance as the target venue, or wants a comparative policy-and-institutions journal rather than a pure PA or pure comparative-politics outlet.
- A single-country study of a reform, executive, or bureaucracy needs re-framing as a case *of* something general.
- A public-administration paper's real contribution is about institutions and executive politics, not management practice.
- The author needs Governance's desk-reject risks and a credible alternative list (CPS, BJPS, PA, JPART, PMR, IPMJ, World Politics).

## Scope & topic fit

- Executive politics: cabinets, presidents and prime ministers, political–administrative relations, politicisation of bureaucracies, agencies and delegation.
- The organization of the state: administrative traditions, state capacity, regulatory and independent bodies, multilevel and international governance arrangements.
- Comparative public policy and policy processes: agenda-setting, policy change and diffusion, implementation across systems, policy instruments.
- Governance quality, corruption, accountability, and institutional reform, especially outside the usual Anglo-American and Western European cases.
- Historical-institutional, qualitative-comparative, quantitative cross-national, and mixed designs are all in remit; corporate governance is explicitly out of scope.

## Method & evidence reporting boundary

- Report the active `empirical-research` core contract ID and revision and, as applicable, its registered estimand or theoretical target, assumptions, diagnostic dispositions, hash-bound Artifact IDs, and observed inference ceiling.
- Explain why those already-registered records matter to this venue's audience. This venue profile does not select estimators, clustering levels, thresholds, diagnostics, or repairs.
- If a required core record is absent or failed, return the design to `empirical-research` and report the resulting narrowed or withdrawn inference; do not repair it in the venue layer.
- Keep the venue-specific placement, data/code, disclosure, and transparency requirements below.

## Structure & house style

- Make the case directly: the editors' published advice is that the title (within about twelve words), the opening of the abstract, and an introduction of no more than about two pages should each state the main claim or finding; never end the introduction by promising conclusions later.
- Journal writing is technical writing: plain language, no ornamental prose, each section opening with what it will argue.
- The comparative payoff must be explicit: name what the case is a case of, why the mechanism is portable, and what a reader working on a different region learns.
- Theory-first, then evidence: the editors warn against "econometric fishing expeditions" on newly found datasets, against regressions of country-level governance indicators on other country-level indicators, and against frameworks proposed without an empirical demonstration of their superiority.
- Author–date referencing with a separate title page for anonymous review; robustness and case material that would breach the overall length go to supplementary material.
- Public servants and politicians are encouraged to submit alongside academics, but the bar of general theoretical significance applies equally.

## Pre-submission self-check

- [ ] One sentence stating the general claim about policy, administration, or institutions that this paper establishes, and where it travels.
- [ ] The applicable core contract ID/revision, hash-bound diagnostics, and observed inference ceiling are reported without venue-layer alteration.
- [ ] The introduction positions the paper against the comparative policy/PA/institutions literature, not only against one country's scholarship.
- [ ] Title, abstract opening, and introduction each state the main finding; the introduction is about two pages.
- [ ] The manuscript is anonymised with a separate title page and sits under the overall length cap, supplementary material included where needed.

## Common desk-reject triggers

- Hiding, overriding, or rhetorically repairing a failed core diagnostic or observed inference ceiling instead of reporting the registered disposition.
- A new framework, typology, or "research agenda" with no empirical component demonstrating that it outperforms existing accounts.
- A conclusion that implementation, diffusion, or transfer is "complex and shaped by domestic politics" with nothing shown beyond the complication itself.
- Atheoretical data-mining of a new dataset, or cross-national regressions built on coarse country-level governance indicators.
- Corporate governance, bare literature reviews or bibliometric surveys, or a narrowly regional reform study whose broader relevance is never established.

## Re-routing decision

- Large-N or case-based comparative politics without a policy/administration core → `comparative-political-studies`; general political-science significance → `british-journal-of-political-science`; international politics → the `international-organization` or `world-politics` deep packs.
- Management practice inside public organisations → `journal-of-public-administration-research-and-theory` (theory-first) or `public-administration-review` (practice bridge); European administrative scholarship → `public-administration-journal`.
- Reform and service delivery → `public-management-review`; explicitly cross-national management → `international-public-management-journal`; policy-process work with a regional focus → Journal of European Public Policy or Journal of Public Policy.
