# Public Administration (public-administration-journal)

## Journal positioning

Public Administration, founded in 1922 and published by Wiley, is the oldest general journal in the field and the anchor of its European tradition. It publishes on "all facets of public administration, public policy and public management" and states three objectives: international scholarly and practitioner dialogue across PA, public management, public organization theory and policy analysis; critical and comparative analysis of the challenges facing contemporary systems of governing; and theoretically and empirically rigorous research on topics of broad current interest. Compared with the US-centred PAR/JPART pair, its reviewer pool and editorial taste are more European and more pluralist: interpretive, critical, discourse-analytic, historical-institutional, and qualitative-comparative work is at home here alongside quantitative and experimental studies, provided it is theoretically serious and speaks to a "major administrative challenge". It also publishes replication studies as a distinct category.

This skill is a **fit / venue-selection / re-framing** tool. It does not replace the journal's current official submission guidelines. Before submitting, re-check the live author instructions on the Wiley site and the Research Exchange submission portal.

## When to trigger

- The author names Public Administration (Wiley) as the target venue, or wants the European-tradition general journal rather than an American one.
- An interpretive, critical, or qualitative-comparative administrative study needs a top general outlet that will not treat it as second-class.
- A public-policy or governance paper with an administrative core needs re-framing toward "systems of governing" and comparative relevance.
- The author needs this journal's desk-reject risks and a credible alternative list (PAR, JPART, PMR, IPMJ, Governance).

## Scope & topic fit

- Public organization theory and administrative behavior: bureaucratic autonomy, politicisation, accountability, administrative traditions and reform trajectories.
- Public management and public services: performance, collaboration and networks, coproduction, contracting and agencification, digitalisation, crisis and resilience.
- Policy analysis with an administrative or governance mechanism: implementation, regulation, evidence use, policy advice, and the politics–administration interface.
- Comparative and international administration, particularly European, Commonwealth, and multilevel (EU) settings, and theoretically framed single-country studies with comparative implications.
- Critical, interpretive, and historical scholarship on the state and governing, and replication studies of published public-administration findings.

## Method & evidence reporting boundary

- Report the active `empirical-research` core contract ID and revision and, as applicable, its registered estimand or theoretical target, assumptions, diagnostic dispositions, hash-bound Artifact IDs, and observed inference ceiling.
- Explain why those already-registered records matter to this venue's audience. This venue profile does not select estimators, clustering levels, thresholds, diagnostics, or repairs.
- If a required core record is absent or failed, return the design to `empirical-research` and report the resulting narrowed or withdrawn inference; do not repair it in the venue layer.
- Keep the venue-specific placement, data/code, disclosure, and transparency requirements below.

## Structure & house style

- The introduction names the administrative challenge, the theoretical advance, and the substantive insight; the editors say they are "especially interested in manuscripts that deal with major administrative challenges that generate theoretical advances and provide substantive insights".
- Free Format submission: any consistent reference style at first submission, figures and tables in or alongside the text; the journal's published style is APA, applied after acceptance.
- A separate title page carrying author details and the ethics/integrity statements (data availability, funding, conflict of interest, ethics approval), because the journal runs double-blind review; five to six keywords.
- Qualitative and interpretive papers are expected to make their evidentiary basis and interpretive logic explicit for a mixed reviewer pool; quantitative papers should position against the European as well as the US literature.
- The journal participates in Wiley's transparent peer review initiative (reviewer reports and decision letters may be published alongside accepted articles unless the author opts out) and accepts manuscripts previously posted as preprints, with the caveat that preprints can compromise anonymity.
- Non-native English authors may add a native-language abstract at acceptance; poor English can get a manuscript returned before review.

## Verified submission facts (live-checked 2026-09-02; re-check before submitting)

- Publisher & submission system: Wiley; new submissions via the Research Exchange portal at https://authors.wiley.com/journal/PADM (source: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/14679299/homepage/forauthors.html).
- Review model: "The journal operates a double-blind peer review policy. Please anonymize your manuscript and supply a separate title page file"; manuscripts are peer reviewed by at least two anonymous reviewers after Editor-in-Chief screening (source: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/14679299/homepage/forauthors.html).
- Word limit: "There is a 9000 word limit" under Free Format submission (source: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/14679299/homepage/forauthors.html); what the count includes is not itemised on the page, so confirm with the editorial office if close to the cap.
- Fees / APC policy for standard submissions: "This journal is a subscription journal that offers an open access option"; an APC applies only if open access is chosen after acceptance (source: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/14679299/homepage/forauthors.html). No submission fee is stated.
- Data / replication policy: "This journal encourages data sharing" under Wiley's Data Sharing policy, with a data availability statement selected at submission; the journal also publishes a Replication Studies article category (source: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/14679299/homepage/forauthors.html).

## Pre-submission self-check

- [ ] One sentence stating the major administrative challenge addressed and the theoretical advance it generates.
- [ ] The applicable core contract ID/revision, hash-bound diagnostics, and observed inference ceiling are reported without venue-layer alteration.
- [ ] The introduction positions the paper against the international (not only US) public-administration literature and says what a comparative reader gains.
- [ ] The manuscript is anonymised, within 9,000 words, and accompanied by a separate title page with all required statements.
- [ ] For interpretive or qualitative designs, the evidentiary basis and interpretive logic are explicit enough for a mixed reviewer pool.

## Common desk-reject triggers

- Hiding, overriding, or rhetorically repairing a failed core diagnostic or observed inference ceiling instead of reporting the registered disposition.
- A descriptive country report or reform narrative with no theoretical advance and no comparative or "systems of governing" relevance.
- A management-technique paper written for practitioners only, better suited to PMR or PAR.
- Poor English or an unreadable manuscript, which the editorial office returns before review.
- An under-anonymised submission or one that ignores the word cap.

## Re-routing decision

- Practice-bridging US-style public administration → `public-administration-review`; theory-driven public management with identification emphasis → `journal-of-public-administration-research-and-theory`.
- Public-service reform, co-production, and management practice → `public-management-review`; cross-national management comparisons → `international-public-management-journal`.
- Executive politics and institutions with a comparative-politics centre of gravity → `governance-journal`; EU policy process → Journal of European Public Policy; regulation → Regulation & Governance.
