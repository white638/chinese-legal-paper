# Political Science Research and Methods (political-science-research-and-methods)

## Journal positioning

PSRM is the official journal of the European Political Science Association (EPSA), published by Cambridge University Press; EPSA sets the journal's policies. It is a general political-science journal "dedicated to publishing original scholarly work of the highest quality from all subfields", with the distinctive requirement that manuscripts "apply rigorous methods to empirical or theoretical problems". What wins is either a substantive question answered with an unusually careful design, or a methodological contribution (new method, estimator, measurement approach, or software) that solves a real problem in applied political analysis. The journal also has a Research Notes track for compact methods and theory pieces, publishes replications and comments, and enforces a strict replication policy that is checked before publication. It is the natural home for EPSA-conference work and for methods papers that are too applied for Political Analysis but too technical for a general journal.

This skill is a **fit / venue-selection / re-framing** tool. It does not replace the journal's current official submission guidelines. Before submitting, re-check the live author instructions on the Cambridge Core site and the ScholarOne submission system.

## When to trigger

- The author names PSRM as the target venue, or wants an EPSA/European general journal that rewards design and method quality.
- A methods contribution with substantive application is deciding between Political Analysis, PSRM, and a general journal.
- A compact result, a replication, or a new tool with software might fit the Research Notes track rather than a full article.
- The author needs PSRM's desk-reject risks and a credible alternative list (BJPS, CPS, JCR, Political Analysis, APSR/AJPS/JOP).

## Scope & topic fit

- Substantive work in any subfield (comparative, behaviour, institutions, IR, political economy, theory) whose design is a central part of the contribution.
- New or improved methods for applied political analysis: measurement and scaling, text and computational methods, causal-inference tools, survey and experimental design, formal modelling with empirical implications.
- Research Notes: a new method or methodology with software components, a new theoretical idea, or a comment on published PSRM work.
- Replications of published articles, which undergo the usual peer review and appear in the research-notes section.
- Work at the intersection of political science and neighbouring disciplines (economics, statistics, sociology, computer science) when it advances political-science practice.

## Method & evidence reporting boundary

- Report the active `empirical-research` core contract ID and revision and, as applicable, its registered estimand or theoretical target, assumptions, diagnostic dispositions, hash-bound Artifact IDs, and observed inference ceiling.
- Explain why those already-registered records matter to this venue's audience. This venue profile does not select estimators, clustering levels, thresholds, diagnostics, or repairs.
- If a required core record is absent or failed, return the design to `empirical-research` and report the resulting narrowed or withdrawn inference; do not repair it in the venue layer.
- Keep the venue-specific placement, data/code, disclosure, and transparency requirements below.

## Structure & house style

- State up front whether the contribution is substantive, methodological, or both, and choose the track accordingly (Original Article, Research Note, Replication, Comment).
- The inclusive length caps count tables, figures, footnotes, references, and appendices, so the paper is compact by design; simulation and proof material goes in supplementary files that are themselves part of the replication package.
- Methods papers demonstrate the tool on a real political-science problem and say what changes substantively, not only statistically; software is a plus for Research Notes.
- The abstract is very short, and the journal expects funding and competing-interest declarations at the back of the manuscript.
- Replication materials must be sufficient to reproduce every table and figure, including simulation code for theoretical work (agent-based models, Monte Carlo studies); the package is uploaded to the PSRM Dataverse and replicability is assessed by journal staff before publication.
- The default review model is single-anonymous, so anonymisation is optional; authors who prefer a fully anonymous process can request it in the submission system.

## Verified submission facts (live-checked 2026-09-02; re-check before submitting)

- Publisher & submission system: Cambridge University Press for the European Political Science Association; submissions via ScholarOne at http://mc.manuscriptcentral.com/psrm (sources: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/political-science-research-and-methods/information/author-instructions/submitting-your-materials; https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/political-science-research-and-methods/information/author-instructions/preparing-your-materials).
- Review model: "This journal uses a single-anonymized model of peer review. The author does not know the identity of the reviewers, but the reviewers know the identity of the author" (source: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/political-science-research-and-methods/information/peer-review-information/review-process). The Cambridge-hosted Instructions for Contributors adds that authors may opt into double-blind review (source: http://assets.cambridge.org/RAM/RAM_ifc.pdf; confirm this option in the live system).
- Word limit: Original Articles "should not exceed 9,000 words in length, including tables, figures, footnotes, references and appendices"; Research Notes "should not exceed 4,000 words" on the same inclusive basis; abstract "should not exceed 120 words" (source: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/political-science-research-and-methods/information/author-instructions/preparing-your-materials).
- Fees / APC policy for standard submissions: no submission fee is stated; an APC applies only to open-access publication, may be covered by a Cambridge agreement, and can be waived for unfunded authors; Comments carry no APC (source: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/political-science-research-and-methods/information/author-instructions/preparing-your-materials).
- Data / replication policy: authors "must make replication material publicly available at time of publication" via the PSRM Dataverse (https://dataverse.harvard.edu/dataverse/PSRM), sufficient "to replicate results in all tables and figures"; manuscripts are accepted contingent on replicability, initially assessed by PSRM staff (sources: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/political-science-research-and-methods/information/author-instructions/preparing-your-materials; http://assets.cambridge.org/RAM/RAM_ifc.pdf).

- Before giving submission-ready advice, read `../../resources/source-basis.md` and `../../resources/official-source-map.md`; start from the official source anchors for this journal family, then cite the current journal-specific page you checked.
- Search the live site for "Political Science Research and Methods author instructions / preparing your materials" and follow the current Cambridge Core / EPSA version.
- Re-check the article type, the inclusive word caps, the 120-word abstract, reference style, ORCID, and the funding, competing-interest, data-availability, and ethics statements required at the back of the manuscript.
- Re-check the PSRM Dataverse replication requirements, including simulation material for theoretical papers, and whether double-blind review can still be requested.
- If the live official instructions conflict with this skill, the official instructions win.

## Pre-submission self-check

- [ ] One sentence stating the political-science problem and the methodological or design advance that PSRM readers gain.
- [ ] The applicable core contract ID/revision, hash-bound diagnostics, and observed inference ceiling are reported without venue-layer alteration.
- [ ] The introduction positions the paper against both the substantive literature and the relevant methods literature.
- [ ] The manuscript sits under the inclusive cap for its track, with supplementary material listed in the replication package.
- [ ] The PSRM Dataverse package reproduces every table and figure, including simulations.

## Common desk-reject triggers

- Hiding, overriding, or rhetorically repairing a failed core diagnostic or observed inference ceiling instead of reporting the registered disposition.
- A substantive paper whose design is ordinary and whose contribution would be judged on topic alone; PSRM's added value is rigour, not breadth.
- A methods paper with no political-science application or no statement of what the tool changes substantively.
- Text, tables, or figures already published or forthcoming elsewhere, or a manuscript under review at another journal.
- Over-length submissions on the inclusive count, or a replication package that cannot reproduce the exhibits.

## Re-routing decision

- Pure methodology with heavier technical content → Political Analysis; general significance with a substantive focus → `british-journal-of-political-science` or the `apsr` / `ajps` / `jop` deep packs.
- Comparative substantive contributions → `comparative-political-studies`; conflict and cooperation → `journal-of-conflict-resolution`; institutions and policy of the state → `governance-journal`.
- Short general pieces → Research & Politics; European policy and politics → Journal of European Public Policy or European Journal of Political Research.
